# Fishbone cause-and-effect diagram

## Planning

- Put the source-defined problem/effect at the head. Derive major cause categories and their cause/sub-cause hierarchy from the source.
- Do not force generic categories such as People, Process, Equipment, Materials, Environment, or Management unless the source supports them.
- Keep branch labels concise and balance categories across the spine while preserving their hierarchy.

## XML generation

- Draw one horizontal spine pointing to the effect on the right, with major diagonal category branches and smaller sub-cause branches.
- Use straight line relationships with a consistent stroke and restrained arrowheads; this is a cause hierarchy, not an orthogonal flowchart.
- Keep category labels near their branches, prevent branch text collisions, and do not add unrelated connector edges.
